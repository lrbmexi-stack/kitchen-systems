# Server Reference: Halibut Ceviche Tostada

**DRAFT. Illustrative documentation example.**

## The Story

Ceviche is one of the oldest preservation techniques in coastal Latin America. The logic is simple: acid denatures protein the same way heat does. Citrus juice firms the texture, sets the color, and draws out the flavor of whatever goes into it. Every coastal version from Peru to Mexico is built on the same foundation. The proteins change. The acid does not.

This dish runs three proteins and each one gets a different treatment before it ever hits the mix. The halibut is diced, given a brief tamari marinade, then set into an orange and lemon juice bath to cure. Halibut is lean and delicate, so the cure is short and watched closely. The 16/20 prawns are boiled in Old Bay and lemon, chopped, and chilled. The bay scallops are cut small and cured separately in lime and lemon with Aleppo pepper, then folded in last. Each protein lands at a different texture. That is the point.

The base is tomato juice, cocktail sauce, champagne vinegar, roma tomato, red onion, crushed garlic, house pickled jalapeños with their brine, and sambal oelek. That combination pulls the dish toward a Veracruz build rather than a straight Peruvian. More body in the liquid, a chili heat that runs underneath everything. The whole thing sits cold on three fried corn tortillas. Beet aioli on each one first. It is not a garnish. It keeps the tostada from drying out under the slaw and it grounds the dish before the acid hits.

## Flavor Profile

Cold and acid forward. Orange, lemon, and lime hit first. The tomato base and cocktail sauce pull things warmer underneath. Three heat sources: Aleppo pepper in the scallop cure, house pickled jalapeño in the mix, sambal oelek in the base. They layer rather than stack. It builds, it does not spike. The tamari marinade on the halibut puts a savory note in the background you cannot place but can taste. The beet aioli is sweet and earthy at the base. The slaw adds crunch and one more hit of lemon on top.

## Guest FAQs. Know These Cold

- **Is this dish raw?** None of the proteins are raw. The halibut was marinated in soy sauce, then cured in orange and lemon juice until firm. The prawns were boiled in Old Bay and lemon. The scallops were acid cured in lime and lemon with Aleppo. All three proteins are cooked by different methods before the dish is plated.
- **Is this dish spicy?** It has real heat but it is layered, not aggressive. Three sources that build over a few bites. A heat sensitive guest will feel it. A guest who cannot eat any spice should skip this one.
- **Is it gluten free?** Yes, as built. The marinade is tamari, which is fermented soy without wheat, and the tortillas are corn. House rule: for a celiac guest, confirm with the kitchen first so the day's sub recipe brands (cocktail sauce, sambal) get checked before you promise anything.
- **What is halibut?** Firm, lean white fish. Clean, sweet flavor, large flake, nothing fishy about it. A premium fish and a good entry point for a guest hesitant about seafood.
- **What are bay scallops?** Small scallops, about the size of a thumbnail. Sweeter and more tender than sea scallops. Cut small so they distribute evenly through the mix.
- **Can I get it without one of the proteins?** Individual proteins can come out. Check with the kitchen before you promise it. There is no substitute available.
- **Is the beet aioli spicy?** No. Beet juice stock mixed into miso mayo. Sweet and earthy. No heat.

## How to Sell It

- Best opener for a steak table. Cold, acid forward, light on the stomach. It does not compete with what comes after it.
- Lead with the three proteins. Halibut, prawn, and scallop on one plate, each prepared differently. That is the story.
- If a guest is hesitant about raw seafood, close it fast. Nothing in this dish is raw. Walk them through it: halibut is soy marinated and citrus cured, prawns are boiled, scallops are acid cured. That conversation builds confidence and sells the dish.
- Three tostadas on a big plate. Mention that before it lands. The presentation does the work once they see it.
- Natural share for two. One plate, three tostadas, splits clean.

## Pairings

Albarino is the call. The salinity and citrus in the wine mirror the ceviche directly. Muscadet works the same way. Both are low oak, high acid, and they sharpen the dish rather than fight it. Avoid anything oaked or tannic.

Cocktails: go citrus forward. A margarita works. A Paloma works. The acid in the glass amplifies the acid in the dish.

Beer: a light Mexican lager, cold and clean. Do not overthink it.

No alcohol: sparkling water with lime. The carbonation and the citrus do the same job as the wine.
